## 1. Summary

This paper develops and systematically evaluates a systematically tuned **Bayesian Generalized Nonlinear Model (BGNLM)** framework (via `FBMS` and parallel Genetically Modified Mode Jumping MCMC, GMJMCMC) for basis-free discovery of governing ordinary differential equations (ODEs) from noisy time-series measurements. Building on a preliminary three-system workshop prototype (Hubin, 2025), the authors introduce a calibrated pipeline combining unknown-variance Jeffreys-BIC scoring, a complexity-weighted model prior ($r = N^{-1}$), radian-scaled and Michaelis–Menten/Hill physical transforms, disabled projection mutations ($p_{\text{proj}}=0$), a low-dimensional collinearity guard in `FBMS:::check.collinearity`, Median Probability Model (MPM) selection, and post-hoc relative $L_2$-energy pruning ($\tau_{\text{energy}} = 0.025$).

Empirically, the authors present a large-scale synthetic benchmark across **12 dynamical systems** (polynomial, transcendental/rational, and 5D Lorenz-96; 13 methods; $109{,}192$ completed runs) and **5 real-world empirical datasets** ($2{,}600$ runs across two differentiation smoothers and 20 rolling temporal folds), analyzed using Linear Mixed-Effects Models (LMMs). On the synthetic suite, `BGNLM_Full_Tuned` achieves $\text{F1}=0.847$ and the lowest overall false discovery rate ($\text{FDR}=0.102$), closely trailing an oracle-basis Bayesian model ($\text{F1}=0.859$) and ranking #1 on 5D Lorenz-96 ($\text{F1}=0.983$).

While the core methodological motivation—avoiding the fixed-library misspecification/multicollinearity dilemma via Bayesian symbolic feature evolution—is compelling and the engineering effort is substantial, a deep audit of both the manuscript and the underlying codebase reveals **several major methodological, statistical, and experimental-integrity issues** that must be resolved before this work meets ICML standards.

---

## 2. Strengths

1. **Clear Motivation and Diagnosis of Real Failure Modes in `FBMS`:** Identifying and fixing the low-dimensional collinearity check bug in `FBMS:::check.collinearity` ($n_{\text{col}} = 2 p_0$ causing `lm.fit` rank failure once $|\mathcal{F}_j| \ge 4$ in 2D/3D ODEs), switching from degree-scaled to radian-scaled trigonometry, and leaving $\sigma_j^2$ free instead of fixed at $\sigma^2=1$ are concrete, high-impact fixes that genuinely explain the jump from the untuned IWSM baseline ($\text{F1}=0.562$) to tuned BGNLM ($\text{F1}=0.847$).
2. **Scale and Breadth of Empirical Testbed:** Testing across 12 canonical ODEs (spanning quadratic, cubic, trigonometric, rational/Hill, and 5D coupled dynamics) over 5 sample sizes and 7 SNR regimes, complemented by 5 real-world datasets with both Savitzky–Golay spline and TVRegDiff differentiation and forward RK4 trajectory rollouts, provides a rich benchmark infrastructure.
3. **Attempt at Principled Hierarchical Inference:** Moving beyond unclustered summary tables toward mixed-effects modeling and reporting Nogueira selection stability ($\hat{\Phi}$) alongside forward rollout fidelity ($R^2_{\text{traj}}$) and coverage is laudable in principle, even though the current LMM specification has serious flaws detailed below.
4. **Transparent Open Codebase:** Having the full pipeline, manifests, canonicalizer, and aggregated outputs available enables complete reproducibility and verification.

## 3. Major Weaknesses

### W1. Baseline Integrity Gap: `SR_PySR` and `SR_Operon` Did Not Run Native PySR or PyOperon (Nor Did `UQ_SINDy` / `SINDy_PI` Run Standard Algorithms)
- **Evidence:** The Abstract (line 49), Introduction (line 64), and Section 3.1 (line 182) state that the paper compares against *"genetic-programming symbolic regression (PySR, PyOperon)"* \citep{cranmer2023interpretable,burlacu2020operon} with Pareto knee selection (`best`) and Levenberg–Marquardt + MDL refinement. However, inspecting `scripts/run_remote_server.sh` and `python/run_sr_pysindy.py` (lines 290–384) reveals that native `PySRRegressor` and `pyoperon` only execute if `PYSR_USE_JULIA=1` and `PYOPERON_NATIVE=1` are set. Neither flag is set in `run_remote_server.sh`. Inspecting `results/aggregated/synth_results.rds` and `real_results.rds` confirms the fallback branch ran:
  - Both `SR_PySR` and `SR_Operon` average $4.4$–$6.1\,\text{s}$ per system (far too fast to cold-start Julia PySR per equation) and emit template atoms exclusive to the Python fallback pool `generate_candidate_atoms()` (e.g., `x1*x2^2/(1+x2^2)` on `real04_cascaded_tanks`, `exp(-abs(x))` via `exp(x)`, and identical `eq1::x1;...;eq5::x5` outputs across both SR methods on 5D Lorenz-96).
  - Thus, what is labeled `SR_PySR` and `SR_Operon` in all tables, figures, and LMM contrasts is actually an in-house ~80-line Python greedy OMP + random subset mutation heuristic (`fit_evolutionary_pareto_sr`) over a static ~40-column precomputed pool (where `method="lm"` in line 156 optimizes an already exact linear OLS solution).
- **Similarly for `UQ_SINDy` and `SINDy_PI_Rational` (`R/04_sindy_baselines.R`):**
  - `UQ_SINDy` (lines 204–264) does **not** implement the hierarchical Bayesian spike-and-slab / regularized horseshoe MCMC of Hirsh et al. (2022); it implements 80-replicate STLSQ bagging (Fasel et al., 2022) with an ad-hoc hard threshold `lam_base = max(0.03, 0.25 * median(|beta_active|))`—cutting the CV-BIC threshold by $4\times$, which directly explains its inflated FDR ($0.406$–$0.490$) and poor PIP Brier score ($0.260$).
  - `SINDy_PI_Rational` (lines 96–134) does **not** solve implicit SINDy-PI equations $g(\boldsymbol{x}, \dot{\boldsymbol{x}}) = 0$ (Kaheman et al., 2020); it runs explicit `cv_stlsq_fit` on `Poly(2)` plus 6 hand-picked rational templates.
- **Required Fix:** Either run genuine native `PySR` and `PyOperon` (and true Bayesian UQ-SINDy / implicit SINDy-PI) on the benchmark—or if computational cost prevents that on 109k runs, **rename and accurately describe these baselines** (e.g., `SR_OMP_Pareto`, `SR_OMP_MDL`, `Bagging_STLSQ`, `STLSQ_Rational`) and add a head-to-head comparison against genuine `PySR` / `PyOperon` / Bayesian `UQ-SINDy` on a representative subset. Attributing results of a custom Python OMP proxy to Cranmer (2023) and Burlacu et al. (2020) is unacceptable for ICML.

### W3. Bayesian Formulation, Post-Hoc Pruning Coherence, and Predictive Interval Construction
1. **Jeffreys-BIC Marginal Likelihood Precision (Section 2.2, line 105):**
   - Assigning the improper prior $p(\beta_{j0}, \boldsymbol{\beta}_{j,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_j}, \sigma_j^2 \mid \boldsymbol{\gamma}_j) \propto \sigma_j^{-2}$ does **not** produce an "exact" marginal likelihood across models of different dimensions $p_{\gamma}$, because the flat improper prior on $\boldsymbol{\beta}_{j,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_j} \in \mathbb{R}^{p_\gamma}$ carries an arbitrary dimension-dependent normalizing constant $c^{p_\gamma}$ (the Jeffreys–Lindley paradox). Rather, BIC approximates the marginal likelihood under a proper **unit-information prior** (Kass & Wasserman, 1995) up to $O(1)$.
   - Also, line 105 writes $-\frac{N}{2}\log(\text{RSS}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}_j}) - \frac{p_{\gamma}}{2}\log N + O(1)$, omitting the $-\frac{N}{2}\log(\text{RSS}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}_j}/N)$ scaling (i.e., $+\frac{N}{2}\log N$ constant) and the intercept degree of freedom (`FBMS:::gaussian.loglik` actually computes `-(mod$aic + (log(N)-2)*mod$rank - 2*log(r)*sum(complex$oc))/2`, where `mod$rank` is $p_\gamma + 1$).
2. **Complexity Prior Normalization & Double-Penalization (Eq. 3, line 99):**
   - Combining Jeffreys-BIC with $r = N^{-1}$ means adding a feature $g_k$ of complexity $c(g_k)$ pays **both** $\frac{1}{2}\log N$ (from BIC) and $c(g_k)\log N$ (from Eq. 3), totaling $(c(g_k) + 0.5)\log N$. Moreover, writing $p(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_j \mid \mathcal{F}_j) \propto \prod_k r^{c(g_k)\gamma_{jk}}$ treats the evolved feature population $\mathcal{F}_j$ as fixed rather than explicitly addressing how GMJMCMC renormalizes across dynamically changing populations $\mathcal{F}_j^{(p)} \subset \mathcal{G}_D$.
3. **Expressivity Blind Spot from Disabling Projections ($p_{\text{proj}} = 0$, $D \le 2$):**
   - Disabling projections cleanly prevents nested unidentifiable trees, **but** forces all nonlinearities to use unit internal parameters (e.g., $\sin(x_1)$, $1/(1+x_3^2)$, $x_3/(1+x_3)$). BGNLM with $p_{\text{proj}}=0$ and $D \le 2$ **cannot** discover non-unit frequencies $\sin(\omega x_1)$ ($\omega \neq 1$), Michaelis–Menten constants $K_m \neq 1$, or general exponential rates $e^{-a x_1}$ ($a \neq 1$). Notably, all non-polynomial synthetic systems in Group B (`sys08`–`sys11` with $K_m=1.0$) and the real-world reference atoms (`Paramecium` $1+x_1$, `Yeast` $1+x_2^2$) happen to have unit internal scale constants, favorably masking this limitation.
4. **Post-Hoc $L_2$-Energy Pruning Breaks Bayesian Coherence & PIP Consistency (Eq. 4, lines 120–122, `R/03_fbms_wrappers.R` lines 336–360):**
   - After MPM selection ($\widehat{\text{PIP}}_{jk} > 0.5$), the pipeline fits frequentist OLS (`lm.fit`), deterministically discards terms with $|\hat{\beta}_{jk}|\|g_k(\boldsymbol{X})\|_2 / \|\boldsymbol{y}_j\|_2 < 0.025$, and refits OLS.
   - This violates Bayesian coherence in two ways: (i) a genuine physical term with $\widehat{\text{PIP}}=0.99$ and weak signal contribution ($<2.5\%$ $L_2$ norm) is hard-pruned regardless of sample size or posterior certainty; (ii) `pip_list` retains the *unpruned* PIPs, so the reported PIP Brier score evaluates probabilities that do not match the final pruned model $\hat{\mathcal{S}}_j$.
5. **Ad-Hoc Predictive Intervals & Code–Text Mismatch (`R/03_fbms_wrappers.R` lines 388–430 vs. line 123):**
   - Section 2.3 (line 123) describes combining BMA epistemic quantiles with a *"Student-$t$/normal residual predictive standard error"* in quadrature. Inspecting `R/03_fbms_wrappers.R` reveals three issues:
     - First, **no Student-$t$ distribution is used**: lines 412 and 424 hardcode the normal quantile `1.96`, not `qt(0.975, df_res)`.
     - Second, intervals are centered at the **post-pruning frequentist OLS mean** `mu_in`/`mu_ood`, not the BMA mean (`bma_in$aggr$mean`), while `bma_halfwidth` is taken from unpruned `predict(fbms_fit)`.
     - Third, `se_in = sigma_hat * sqrt(1 + g^T (G^T G + 1e-6 I)^{-1} g)` **already includes both aleatoric variance and within-model parameter variance**, and `FBMS:::predict.gmjmcmc_merged` computes `aggr$quantiles` as a weighted average of per-population quantiles over deterministic model point predictions `yhat[, k]`. Adding `bma_halfwidth` and `1.96 * se_in` in quadrature around a post-selection OLS mean is an ad-hoc hybrid rather than a coherent Bayesian mixture predictive distribution. (And notice OOD coverage still drops to $87.7\%$ at $N=2500, \sigma=0.001$ due to unmodeled structural bias.)

### W4. Misdiagnosed $N=2500$ F1 Drop (`0.883 -> 0.760`) — Direct Contradiction with Empirical Data
- **Claim in Manuscript (Lines 308 and 430):** The paper attributes the drop in `BGNLM_Full_Tuned` F1 from $0.883$ ($N=500$) to $0.760$ ($N=2500$) to *"the $r=N^{-1}$ penalty becoming slightly conservative on weak non-polynomial terms like $0.2\sin(x_1)$ under a fixed $N_{\text{MCMC}}=500$ budget."*
- **What the Data Actually Show (`results/aggregated/synth_results.rds`):**
  - On `sys08_rossler_lorenz_hybrid` (the system containing $0.2\sin(x_1)$), the recovery rate of `sin(x1)` actually **increases** from $59.3\%$ at $N=500$ to $62.9\%$ at $N=2500$, and `TPR` increases ($0.918 \to 0.934$)!
  - Rather than under-selection (conservatism), the $N=2500$ drop is caused by a **$3.4\times$ explosion in False Discovery Rate (`FDR`: $0.063 \to 0.214$)** and bloated active supports across both polynomial and non-polynomial systems: `sys10_thomas_attractor` ($\text{F1}: 0.851 \to 0.322$, $\text{FDR}: 0.186 \to 0.766$, mean active terms $8.56 \to 16.41$ vs. $6$ true!), `sys07_brusselator` ($\text{F1}: 0.968 \to 0.731$, $\text{FDR}: 0.039 \to 0.322$), `sys09_damped_pendulum` ($\text{F1}: 0.989 \to 0.769$, $\text{FDR}: 0.005 \to 0.272$), `sys05_van_der_pol` ($\text{F1}: 0.996 \to 0.789$, $\text{FDR}: 0.005 \to 0.258$), and `sys06_duffing` ($\text{F1}: 0.960 \to 0.793$, $\text{FDR}: 0.023 \to 0.238$), even at near-zero noise $\sigma=0.001$!
  - **Actual Mechanism:** Under Jeffreys-BIC, the log-likelihood term scales as $O(N)$ while the complexity prior scales only as $O(\log N)$. When a GMJMCMC chain with fixed `N_mcmc=500, pop.max=20` has not yet discovered the exact degree-3 or transcendental term (e.g., `sin(x2)` or `x1^2*x2`), $N=2500$ magnifies tiny residual MSE gains of multi-term Taylor/rational surrogates (e.g., `x2^2/(1+x2^2) + x2^6/(1+x2^2) + x2^8/(1+x2^2) + x2^10/(1+x2^2)`) by hundreds of nats, trapping chains in bloated local modes that flood MPM selection. This directly refutes the paper's claim of "asymptotic selection consistency" under fixed MCMC budget and must be diagnosed accurately.

### W5. Calibration Discrepancies, Leakage Concerns, and Experimental Asymmetries
1. **Calibration Description Mismatches (`main.tex` Section 2.4 vs. `R/07_tuning_engine.R` & `config/best_bgnlm_params.yaml`):**

   - In Table 1 (`tab:bgnlm_tuning`), `C04` (`Jeffreys-BIC`) beats `C01`/`C05`/`C06` (`g-prior`) on **only 1 of the 6 tasks** (`sys08` at $\sigma=0.01$, single seed `101`), while `C02` (`hyper-g/n`) returned all zeros (`F1=0, R2=0`, likely due to `BAS` interface/numerical failure rather than meaningful prior behavior).
   - **Leakage / Inductive Bias:** Although test seeds differ from tuning seed `101`, all 3 calibration systems remain inside the 12-system benchmark without reporting performance on the 9 held-out systems separately. More importantly, `transforms_extended` (`inv_sq`, `hill_2`, `sin_rad`, `p05`) was curated specifically around the exact benchmark nonlinearities (Goodwin, Pendulum, Thomas, Tanks, Yeast).
2. **Unfair Oracle Framing & Overcomplete/Failed-Run Imputation:**
   - On Groups A and C, `BGLM_Oracle_Basis` is identical to `BGLM_Poly_Basis` ($\mathcal{P}_3(\boldsymbol{X})$). On 5D Lorenz-96 (quadratic true RHS), `BGLM_Oracle_Basis` is burdened with all $55$ cubic terms rather than the true $\mathcal{P}_2$ oracle ($20$ terms), artificially making `BGNLM_Full_Tuned` appear to "beat the oracle" ($\text{F1}=0.983$ vs. $0.948$). Meanwhile on Group B, where true oracle terms actually differ from polynomials, `BGLM_Oracle_Basis` beats `BGNLM_Full_Tuned` by a wide margin ($\text{F1}=0.881$ vs. $0.786$, $\text{Exact}=69.8\%$ vs. $47.0\%$, interaction contrast $+0.125, p<10^{-4}$).
   - When `BGLM_Overcomplete` fails/crashes ($656$ synth runs; $40$ `Paramecium` $n<p$ folds), `scripts/run_single_task.R` imputes `F1=0, R2=0, FDR=0, OC=0`. Imputing `FDR=0` and `OC=0` on crashed runs artificially lowers `BGLM_Overcomplete`'s reported average FDR and OC!
   - **Compute Budget Asymmetry:** 6-chain GMJMCMC spends $235.4\,\text{s}$ (synth) and $137.3\,\text{s}$ (real) per system vs. $1.6$–$2.8\,\text{s}$ for SINDy and $4.4$–$6.1\,\text{s}$ for the SR proxy ($40\times$–$120\times$ runtime difference) without a compute-matched comparison.
   - **Non-Comparable `PIP_Brier` Metric:** `compute_pip_brier_score` (`R/06_metrics_consistency.R` lines 75–104) averages squared PIP errors over the union of candidate columns + true atoms. For `BGLM_Overcomplete` ($60$–$275+$ columns, mostly trivial true negatives with $\text{PIP}\approx 0$), `PIP_Brier` is diluted to `0.0309`, beating `BGNLM_Full_Tuned` (`0.0898`) solely due to denominator inflation!

## 6. Overall Recommendation & Score

- **Score:** **4 / 10** (Borderline Reject — *Major Revision Required before Acceptance*)
- **Confidence:** **5 / 5** (Verified directly against manuscript, scripts, package internals, and aggregated benchmark outputs)

**Rationale:** The methodological core—systematically fixing and calibrating BGNLM/GMJMCMC for basis-free dynamical system discovery—yields strong empirical performance and addresses a genuine limitation of fixed-dictionary SINDy. However, the submission currently suffers from four critical issues that undermine scientific validity in an ICML paper: (i) reporting results from an internal Python OMP proxy as `PySR` and `PyOperon` (and bagging STLSQ as Hirsh et al.'s `UQ-SINDy`); (ii) omitting `(1|System:Method)` / `(1|Dataset:Method)` in the LMMs (causing severe pseudo-replication and hiding 3 singular fits in Experiment 2) while reporting unadjusted p-values despite claiming Dunnett correction; (iii) misattributing the $N=2500$ F1 drop to prior conservatism when the data show a $3.4\times$ FDR explosion; and (iv) post-hoc pruning / predictive interval / calibration discrepancies between the text and code. If the authors address these statistical, methodological, and baseline-integrity flaws rigorously, this has strong potential to become a compelling ICML paper.
